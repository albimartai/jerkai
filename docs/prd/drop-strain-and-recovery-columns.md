## 0. Scope Decisions (read first)

### 0.1 Decision — the two columns are deleted, not relocated or hidden (Albert's explicit direction; DL-2026-09-13-a3)

Considered: (A) hide the two columns at the render layer only (`app/ui/weekly-ledger.tsx` stops rendering them) while `buildWeeklyLedger` keeps computing `strain`/`recovery` cells server-side, unused; (B) remove them at the source — delete `strainCell`/`recoveryCell`, the `strainLevel`/`recoveryLevel` `LedgerCell` variants, `strainRaw`/`recoveryRaw` from `LedgerInput`, and narrow `buildWeeklyLedger`'s signature to drop its `recoveryCfg` parameter entirely. Albert chose (B), shown that (A) would leave dead server-side computation with no consumer and would fail this repo's own standing rule against speculative unused surface (`docs/prd/archive/weekly-ledger.md`'s `NFR-23`: "do not add speculative fields beyond what [the columns] need" — the converse of that rule is not to *keep* fields nothing needs). Build (B): a future Engine page re-derives this aggregation from its own requirements rather than un-deleting this shape.

### 0.2 Decision — gap-week detection narrows to the three remaining series (Albert's direction, confirmed this session; DL-2026-09-13-a4)

`isEmptyWeek` (`lib/dashboard/ledger.ts:149-158`) currently collapses a week to a single gap row only when **all five** series (`bodyFatRaw`, `weightRaw`, `strainRaw`, `recoveryRaw`, `leanMassRaw`) have zero raw readings that week. Once `strainRaw`/`recoveryRaw` are removed from `LedgerInput` entirely (§0.1), this check can only run over the three remaining series — there is no other option once the fields are gone, but the *consequence* is a genuine, testable behavior change and is named here rather than left implicit: a week with Whoop data but no body-fat/weight/lean-mass readings that week — impossible to construct today without Strain/Recovery masking it, but newly possible in the three-series world — now collapses to a gap row, where previously the presence of Strain or Recovery data alone would have kept it from collapsing. This is the correct behavior for a page that no longer renders those two series (§4.2, `AC-W33`) — a "gap" should mean "nothing this page shows happened this week" — but it is a real change from today's shipped behavior, not a no-op refactor, and is called out here so a build agent does not treat it as incidental.

### 0.3 Landmine — `lib/dashboard/types.ts` and `lib/dashboard/strain.ts` are vendor-locked by `jerkai-mcp`; this slice does not touch either

Confirmed live this session (`docs/codebase.md` §6): the sibling repo `jerkai-mcp` carries byte-pinned copies of `lib/dashboard/types.ts` and `lib/dashboard/strain.ts`, checked against a locked commit SHA. Neither file is in this slice's budget (§1) — `DashboardData`'s `series.dayStrain`/`series.recoveryScore` fields stay exactly as they are (`/daily` still reads them directly), and `strain.ts`'s `STRAIN_DOMAIN`/`strainFraction` (used only by `/daily`'s chart rendering, confirmed live this session via `app/ui/dashboard.tsx:12`) are untouched. This slice removes only the ledger's own, separate `strainCell`/`recoveryCell` functions in `lib/dashboard/ledger.ts` — a different module from `strain.ts`, despite the similar name.

### 0.4 Landmine — `RecoveryConfig` is not deleted; only the ledger's use of it is

`lib/dashboard/config.ts`'s `RecoveryConfig` type (`redBelowPct`, `windowDays`) is consumed by two call sites today: `lib/dashboard/ledger.ts`'s `recoveryCell` (removed by this slice) and `lib/dashboard/readouts.ts`'s `recoveryReadout` (used by `/daily`'s guardrail readout row, confirmed live this session via `app/ui/dashboard.tsx:482`, untouched). `RecoveryConfig` itself, and `DASHBOARD_CONFIG.recovery`, stay in `config.ts` exactly as they are — only `buildWeeklyLedger`'s own `recoveryCfg` parameter (which narrows the type down to `Pick<RecoveryConfig, "redBelowPct">`) is deleted, because nothing inside the ledger needs it anymore.

What this slice is NOT: no change to `/daily`'s Strain or Recovery strips, `lib/dashboard/strain.ts`, `lib/dashboard/readouts.ts`, `lib/dashboard/types.ts`, or `lib/dashboard/data.ts`/`fetchDashboardData` (still fetches `dayStrain`/`recoveryScore` for `/daily`'s sake); no new page; no route change (sibling PRD); no schema change, no migration.

## 2. Problem statement

The Weekly Ledger's landing page renders five columns — body fat, weight, Day Strain, Recovery, and lean mass — but Albert wants it to read purely as a body-composition surface, with training/recovery data moved conceptually to a future Engine page rather than left on this one in the interim. Nothing about the three retained columns' computation changes; `strainCell`/`recoveryCell` and the two `LedgerCell` variants they produce are deleted, `buildWeeklyLedger`'s signature narrows, and gap-week detection is redefined over the three series that remain (§0.2) — a real, testable behavior change, not a rendering-only tweak.

## 3. Objective

A user viewing the Weekly Ledger sees exactly three columns per row — body fat trend Δ, weight Δ, lean mass Δ — with no Strain or Recovery data anywhere on the page. `/daily`'s own Strain and Recovery strips are visually and behaviorally identical to today, since they never went through the ledger. Success = a single testable condition: `buildWeeklyLedger`'s output type carries no `strain`/`recovery` field, and the rendered ledger table has exactly three data columns beside the week label.

### 4.1 Column removal

* **AC-W32 (bare case)** — Given the ledger renders any row, Then that row shows exactly three metric columns — body fat trend Δ, weight Δ, lean mass Δ, in that order — and no Strain or Recovery cell, column header, or value anywhere on the page. (Amends the predecessor Weekly Ledger PRD's `AC-W3` in place, §0 header.)
* **AC-W33** — Given a week has data for body fat, weight, or lean mass but none for Strain or Recovery, Then that week is **not** a gap row (unaffected by this slice — those three series alone already determine gap status). Given a week has data for Strain or Recovery only, and none for body fat, weight, or lean mass, Then that week **is** a gap row (`isGap: true`) — the reverse of today's shipped behavior, where Strain/Recovery presence alone would have kept it from collapsing (§0.2).
